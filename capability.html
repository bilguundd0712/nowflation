NOWFLATION.MN / capability note

← the nowcast · aimag demo

What this is

Nowflation.mn is an independent weekly read of Mongolian food and fuel prices, computed from the National Statistics Office's own open weekly price survey and published ahead of each monthly CPI print. Every figure is generated from the source tables at build time — nothing is entered by hand — and every prediction we publish is dated, banded and graded in public against the official release (the append-only calls ledger).

It is a nowcast, not an official statistic. It does not replace the NSO consumer price index and makes no claim to reproduce it.

What already runs

  • The UB nowcast — meat basket, flour and fuel, weekly, with year-on-year, four-survey momentum, and the lead over the official print visualised.
  • The aimag monitor (working demo) — the same survey across all 21 aimags: beef, petrol, diesel and baled hay, against Ulaanbaatar. In the latest week the same cut of beef varies more than 50% between aimags.
  • The scored-calls ledger — every food-CPI prediction dated before the release, graded automatically after it, never edited. The git history of the public repository is the tamper-evidence.

What can be built on it

Aimag price dashboards

Any basket, any aimags, any cadence — from the weekly survey that already covers all 21 aimags. Delivered as a page, a feed, or both.

Early-warning monitors

Threshold-based alerts on the series that matter in a hard winter — baled hay, meat, flour, fuel — with honest staleness rules and no interpolation.

Full-basket builds

The UB survey carries 31 products (dairy, vegetables, bread, eggs); the national CPI tables carry every CoICOP group monthly back to 1993. Custom cuts on request.

Methodology & validation work

Documented, reproducible pipelines on official open data, validated against the official prints — the discipline this site runs on, applied to your series.

How we work

  • All source data is NSO open data (data.1212.mn, PXWeb), licensed CC BY 4.0 and attributed on every page.
  • Builds are deterministic and public where the deliverable is public; failed fetches degrade to cache and say so — a stale page that admits it beats a fresh-looking one.
  • Claims are falsifiable by design: predictions carry bands and grade themselves against the official number.
